Designing a Real-Time SLAM Stack on Embedded Linux

March 14, 2026

When you move SLAM from a development workstation to an embedded target, the conversation shifts from algorithm accuracy to timing guarantees. Every sensor callback, every IPC hop, and every memory allocation becomes a line item in your latency budget.

Partitioning the Stack

I split the navigation stack into three RTOS tasks: sensor acquisition at 100 Hz, fusion at 100 Hz, and planning at 20 Hz. The Linux side handles logging, diagnostics, and non-critical visualization over a shared-memory ring buffer.

IPC That Doesn't Surprise You

Message queues work well for state snapshots, but only if you enforce fixed-size messages and pre-allocated pools. Dynamic allocation inside the fusion loop was the source of a 3 ms jitter spike that took a week to trace.
